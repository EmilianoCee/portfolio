Pipelined RISC-V CPU

Spring 2026 · Logisim, RISC-V ISA, Digital Logic

I built a multi-stage pipelined CPU in Logisim that runs RISC-V instructions. I designed the ALU, register file, branch comparator, and datapath as separate pieces, then put them together into a working pipeline. Most of the real work lived in the seams between those pieces: I traced timing and hazard issues between pipeline stages and worked through them until the datapath stayed correct on every instruction, not just the easy ones. Building a processor from the gate level up changed how I read code, because I can now picture what the hardware is actually doing underneath an instruction instead of treating it as a black box.

Guitar Amplifier and Effects Chain

Fall 2025 · Analog Circuit Design, Op-Amps, Lab Instrumentation

I built a full analog signal chain that takes a guitar all the way to a speaker, testing and fixing each stage on the bench along the way. The chain includes the volume and tone controls, a treble boost pedal, and a power amp, all built around op-amps. When a stage was not behaving the way I expected, I used KVL/KCL and filter design to work out on paper what had to be wrong, then confirmed it with lab equipment before changing anything. Hearing a circuit I designed come through a speaker was the clearest proof I have had that the theory and the bench actually line up.

MyShake Earthquake Early Warning

January 2025 – Present · Swift, SwiftUI, MapKit · UC Berkeley Seismology Lab

I build features in Swift and SwiftUI for MyShake, the UC Berkeley Seismology Lab's production earthquake early warning app. I built the map view in MapKit that shows live USGS earthquakes with shaking-intensity overlays, and I set up earthquake alerts using Apple's push notification service: a silent push wakes the app, the app pulls the event from the API, and it decides whether to alert based on the user's saved location. I also tracked down a map performance problem where the app was recalculating the whole viewport up to 240 times per pan; adding debouncing cut that down to once per pan and dropped the update frequency from 60–120 Hz to 10 Hz. Working on an app that people rely on during an emergency has made me much more careful about what I put on the main thread.
